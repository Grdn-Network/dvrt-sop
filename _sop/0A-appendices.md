## A.3 Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Advisory | Informational traffic providing warning, planning, status, or expectation. Does not of itself authorise movement, and requires no response unless acknowledgment is specifically requested. |
| Aspect | The indication given by a signal. |
| Call | An initial contact transmission establishing communication with a specific party before passing further information, requests, or instructions. Normally identifies who is being called and who is calling, then awaits acknowledgment. |
| Clearance | A positive instruction authorising movement, entry, occupancy, or route use within stated limits. |
| Consist | The entire train and the cars attached. |
| Contact Point | The point at which Road Crew are expected to swap channels or make first contact with the receiving side. |
| Control Boundary | The point or limit at which one Controller’s authority ends and another’s begins. |
| Coordination Point | The point at which Controllers begin working a handoff, before the train reaches the boundary. |
| Critical Information | Any information capable of immediately affecting movement, authority, occupancy, routing, or operational safety if delayed, omitted, or misunderstood. |
| Dark Territory | An area with no active local Controller or dispatch authority assigned. See Valley Map. |
| Dispatch / RTC | Subdivision-level control responsible for through traffic, inter-area flow, and broader routing. |
| Expedite | Safely move as fast as possible; complete the order promptly or without delay. |
| Foul / Fouling | Equipment blocking a track without occupying it, including standing too close to a switch such that a movement on an adjacent route could strike it. |
| FYSA | For Your Situational Awareness. |
| Guidelines | SOP material that shall be complied with and shall not be excluded during SOP-compliant operations. |
| Heavy | A callsign appendix applied to a train operating near, at, or above the defined heavy-tonnage threshold. Subject to special restrictions, handling considerations, or procedural priority. |
| High Tonnage | A train meeting the current tonnage-sheet threshold, or declared operationally restrictive by crew and accepted as such by Dispatch. |
| Hold / Hold Point | An instruction to remain clear of a stated limit or hold in place until further direction. May be predetermined or given on the spot. |
| Hub | A yard or set of yards operating under one Controller, separate from Dispatch. |
| Hub Control | The title used for the Controller in charge of an operational hub. |
| IAW | In Accordance With. |
| Logistics | Controller responsible for directing the location and job assignment of Road Crew and shunters. |
| Main Track | All tracks connecting one station to another. |
| Other Than Main Track | All tracks within yard limits or sidings, or otherwise not labelled main track. |
| Procedures | General guidelines governing how movements and operations shall be executed. |
| Road Crew | The crew operating a consist on main track. |
| Traffic Advisory | A warning or heads-up call that does not of itself authorise movement. |
| Without Delay | To complete promptly, often urgently. Includes permission to interrupt ongoing conversation, and may carry critical information or movements. |
| With You | Initial contact language denoting that the crew is on that Controller’s channel and ready for direction. |
| XFER | Transfer of control of a train from one Controller or controlled area to another. |
| Yard Crew | The crew operating a locomotive inside a yard and moving cars, ordinarily under a Hub Controller. Also referred to as shunters. |
| Yard Main | A main track running through a yard, ordinarily called by the phonetic for the yard followed by main - the E yard main as “Echo Main” - while still falling under other than main track. |
